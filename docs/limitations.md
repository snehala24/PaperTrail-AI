# Known Limitations & Boundary Conditions

## 1. Document Ingestion Limitations
- **Scanned / Image-Only PDFs**: PyMuPDF extracts programmatic text layers. PDFs consisting entirely of scanned bitmap images without embedded OCR text will generate warnings in `Document.extraction_warnings`. Optical Character Recognition (OCR) is intentionally deferred past the MVP.
- **Complex Multi-Column Math / Inline Equations**: LaTeX mathematical symbols in two-column IEEE/ACM layouts may occasionally be extracted in non-linear reading order if bounding box heuristics fail.

## 2. Chunking Heuristics
- **Section Detection**: Section headers are detected via regex heuristics (e.g. `1. Introduction`, `Abstract`, `Methodology`). Papers with idiosyncratic non-standard heading styles may have chunks grouped under generic headings.
- **Cross-Page Table Spans**: Large tables spanning multiple consecutive pages are currently partitioned per-page rather than merged into a single atomic table chunk.

## 3. Benchmark Limitations & Sample Size
- **Benchmark Annotation Provenance & Verification Status**: The benchmark corpus consists of **30 total questions** (`eval/questions.jsonl`) supported by **34 gold evidence entries** (`eval/gold_evidence.jsonl`), divided into two authoring status tiers:
  - **AI-Draft Partition (10 questions, Q-001–Q-010; 10 gold entries)**: Generated by assistant (`status: "ai_draft"`).
  - **Human-Draft Partition (20 questions, Q-011–Q-030; 24 gold entries)**: Authored by annotator snehala24 (`status: "human_draft"`). This partition comprises 18 single-paper questions (Q-011–Q-028; 20 gold entries) and 2 cross-paper questions (Q-029 and Q-030; 4 gold entries, `GOLD-031`–`GOLD-034`).
  - **Cross-Paper Question Category (Q-029 and Q-030; 4 gold entries)**: Comparative and apparent-conflict queries authored by annotator snehala24 with `status: "human_draft"`. Gold evidence spans (`GOLD-031`–`GOLD-034`, also `status: "human_draft"`) ground both questions across LEMS (`doc_c67573ef50cc`) and GFWSVD (`doc_7fdb1200e823`). Both papers are ingested and indexed in the vector store and BM25 index. As of 2026-11-02 the retrieval runner's two hardcoded exclusions of these questions were removed (`run_retrieval.py`), so Q-029 and Q-030 are now evaluated and the answerable sample is n=25 rather than n=23. Q-030 remains outside the conflict runner: that runner is scripted and offline, and no deterministic mock response is registered for a cross-paper conflict (see below).
  - *Verification Protocol*: None of the partitions have completed the formal expert human verification protocol required to graduate to `status: "human_verified"` (see `eval/LABELLING.md`). All annotations remain unverified drafts (`status: "ai_draft"` or `status: "human_draft"`). The status string `pending_second_paper` does not exist in the evaluation dataset.
- **Single-Paper vs. Multi-Paper Evaluation Scope**: the automated batch retrieval benchmark (`backend/app/eval/run_retrieval.py`) scores every answerable question against the whole five-paper index, including the two cross-paper questions. What it does not do is evaluate multi-paper *answering*: claim minimality, comparison derivation and conflict classification across documents are covered by targeted integration tests (`backend/tests/test_day4_cross_paper.py`, 41 tests) and controlled live end-to-end checks, not by an automated multi-paper generation benchmark. No such runner exists yet.
- **Sample Size and Confidence Intervals**:
  - Current run (`eval/runs/run_20261102_132940.json`, 2026-11-02): the retrieval runner evaluates **$n=25$ answerable queries** (17 researcher-drafted + 8 assistant-drafted) and reserves **5 unanswerable queries** for abstention testing, against the 5-paper / 117-page / 409-chunk corpus. Under hybrid reranking Recall@5 is 0.8800 with a 95% bootstrap interval of [0.74, 0.98] and MRR 0.6927 [0.538, 0.827].
  - Historical run (`eval/runs/run_20261029_073045.json`): **$n=23$ answerable queries** (15 human-draft + 8 AI-draft) on the then-current 2-paper / 209-chunk corpus, with Recall@5 0.8696 and MRR 0.6827 under hybrid reranking. Two hardcoded exclusions of Q-029 and Q-030 were what kept that sample at 23.
  - The two runs are **not** a before/after comparison: corpus size, evaluated population and parser behaviour all changed between them. Neither sample reaches the $n \ge 30$ threshold for a formal significance claim, and the intervals overlap in every configuration.

## 5. Day 4 Cross-Paper Boundary Conditions
- **Asymmetric Cross-Paper Term Overlap**: When querying across multiple papers where Paper A cites or reviews Paper B (e.g. LEMS reviewing GFWSVD), Paper A naturally has higher lexical and semantic term overlap for cross-paper comparative queries. Balanced candidate allocation and multi-document retrieval pooling are applied to prevent evidence starvation from the earlier paper.
- **Source-Aware Grounding vs. Secondary Description**: Claims regarding a specific paper's method should ideally be grounded directly in that paper's own primary chunks rather than in a competing paper's commentary. When primary-source evidence is absent from the cited pool, the system qualifies the response to clarify that claims rely on secondary descriptions rather than independent primary-source evidence.
- **Cross-Paper Comparison Derivation & Unresolved Relations**: When comparing claims across multiple documents, semantic consensus (`supports`), divergence (`apparent_conflict`), or direct contradiction (`contradicts`) must never be inferred solely from distinct document IDs. When the model or evidence does not establish a definitive relationship between verified multi-document claims, the relationship remains unresolved rather than defaulting to agreement or conflict.

## 6. Live Cross-Paper Validation Scope
- **Controlled live validation (2026-10-30)**: one multi-document question ("Do LEMS and GFWSVD describe the Kronecker-factor estimation process in the same way?") against `doc_c67573ef50cc` + `doc_7fdb1200e823` with `top_k=8` returned `answered` with two minimally sufficient claims, each grounded in its own paper's primary chunk, all three verification layers passing, and exactly one comparison classified conservatively as `unresolved`.
- **Validation Scope Caveat**: This is targeted qualitative validation of multi-document retrieval balancing, minimal claim formulation, verification and comparison filtering under a single query. It is not an empirical proof of generalizability across arbitrary multi-paper queries.

## 7. Remaining Limitations & Future Work
- **Automated Multi-Paper Conflict Batch Coverage**: `run_retrieval.py` now scores Q-029 and Q-030 like any other question. `run_conflicts.py` still reports Q-030 as blocked, and deliberately so: it is a deterministic offline runner that answers every question through a scripted `MockLLMProvider` response, and no scripted response is registered for a genuine two-paper conflict. Fabricating one would score the script rather than the product, so the question stays out of the denominator and the case is covered by live end-to-end validation instead.
- **No Verified Genuine Contradiction In This Corpus**: an exhaustive offline mine of all 409 indexed chunks — 302 opposition-bearing sentences, 30 cross-document candidate pairs at cosine similarity ≥ 0.62 — produced no pair satisfying the four criteria for a genuine contradiction (same proposition, opposing conclusion, comparable conditions, verbatim support from both sides). The five papers largely agree; the closest pairs are methodological differences that correctly classify as apparent conflict. Contradiction *detection* is therefore unproven on this corpus, and the pipeline's behaviour is only evidenced for conflicts handed to it in tests. See [benchmark-contradictions.md](benchmark-contradictions.md).
- **Annotation Verification Graduation**: Benchmark items remain drafts and none has been signed off against the PDFs. `eval/questions.jsonl` holds 30 questions (20 `human_draft`, 10 `ai_draft`); `eval/gold_evidence.jsonl` holds 34 spans (24 `human_draft` labelled by the researcher, 10 `ai_draft` labelled by the assistant). `status: "human_verified"` count is 0. `eval/LABELLING.md` is the protocol, `eval/HUMAN-REVIEW-GUIDE.md` is the reviewer's instruction set, and `backend/app/eval/review_worksheet.py` generates one checklist per gold item. Protocol `1.1` resolves each span against the stored corpus into `exact` / `case only` / `hyphen or space repair` / `other page` / `absent` / `no document`: the current run reports 33 `exact` and 1 `case only` (GOLD-010 records `Integer Linear Program` where page 5 prints `Integer linear program (ILP)`), with 15 of 34 items carrying a review caution — 2 `contradicts` labels whose own `conflict_reason` text describes an apparent conflict, 2 spans of 5–7 characters, 4 cross-paper items, 7 conflict-typed questions whose spans are labelled `supports`, and GOLD-025/GOLD-033 recording the identical page-6 span. Structural integrity is not equivalent to human validation: none of the 34 has been read against a PDF and signed off, so no accuracy number is published as human-graded.
- **Upstream Provider Limits**: Gemini's free tier enforces strict per-day and per-minute request caps. When a request is refused or times out, PaperTrail AI's zero-tolerance verification policy fails closed by abstaining rather than emitting unverified claims; the user sees an explicit provider failure, not a silent one. Production use requires a paid quota or a locally hosted verification model.
- **Corpus Scaling and Ingestion Modalities**: retrieval is now measured at the current scale — 5 papers, 117 pages, 409 chunks (`eval/runs/run_20261102_132940.json`, $n=25$). Cross-paper balancing and minimal claim formulation remain targeted qualitative validation on a single query rather than a batch-scored capability. Performance on corpora with noisy OCR scans, very large context pools, or substantially more papers remains unproven.
- **Stored Document Metadata Predates The Parser Fix**: page-1 title extraction was corrected on 2026-11-02 (line-based selection with small-caps and mid-word hyphen handling), and the figure-only warning no longer counts pages whose chunking failed. Four of the five stored titles are already correct; one record — LoRA `doc_e9a0d3128767` — stores `L RA: L -R A L L - M`, the largest-font heuristic collecting single glyphs from a letter-spaced small-caps title. The frontend compensates with a one-entry display override table (`frontend/src/lib/display.ts`), applied through `documentDisplayTitle()` and the `DocumentCatalogueProvider`, so the corrected title reaches every view; the fallback for an unknown garbled record is the filename, never an invented title.
- **Re-ingestion Is Not Recommended For That Record** (assessed 2026-11-02, no corpus change made). Running the corrected extractor read-only against `data/2106.09685v2.pdf` yields `LORA: LOW-RANK ADAPTATION OF LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS` — correct but all-caps, which reads worse than the override table's `LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models`, and the PDF carries no metadata title to fall back on. All five documents are already indexed by chunker `0.2.3`, the version the code still runs, so re-ingestion would not improve chunking either. What it would do is rewrite the five records in `data/` and rebuild the FAISS index for a cosmetic metadata field — the one change the display layer already handles. The workaround therefore stays, and is revisited only if the corpus is re-ingested for some other approved reason. One related finding: `doc_7fdb1200e823` stores its metadata title ("Generalized Fisher-Weighted SVD: … for Compressing Large Language Models") while its page 1 prints a different title ("Scalable Kronecker-Factored Fisher Approximation for Neural Network Parameter Sensitivity"). Metadata takes precedence in the parser, so re-ingestion would keep the stored value; the divergence is the paper's own arXiv revision history, not an extraction bug, and is recorded here so nobody later "fixes" it by accident.

- **Misattribution Guard Cannot See The LoRA Name** (found while assessing re-ingestion; a functional consequence, not a display one). `_extract_doc_identifiers()` in `backend/app/claims/service.py` harvests uppercase acronyms from a document's title and chunk text (`\b[A-Z]{2,10}\b`, `\b[A-Z]{3,10}\b`) to decide whether a cross-paper comparison refers to a paper not represented in its own evidence. Mixed-case abbreviations match neither pattern, so "LoRA" is invisible to the acronym paths. Title words of four characters or more are a second chance — which is why DoRA's intact title still yields `dora` — but the garbled LoRA title has no such token, so that rescue is lost too. Measured against the live store, the LoRA record resolves to `doc_e9a0d3128767`, `e9a0d3128767`, the `doc …`/`paper …` variants, the filename stem `2106.09685v2`, and one spurious `ra` — and not to "lora". It fails in the safe direction: it can under-suppress a misattributed comparison, never invent one, and a comparison still has to clear the verbatim-citation layers first. Widening the patterns would also admit common model names (LLaMA, GPT) as identifiers and start suppressing legitimate comparisons, so this is left as a test-first follow-up rather than changed during release preparation. Re-ingesting LoRA would incidentally improve it (an all-caps page-1 title yields `lora`, `rank`, `adaptation`, `language`, `models`), which is the one non-cosmetic argument for re-ingestion — weighed above against rewriting five indexed records and rebuilding the FAISS index, and declined.
